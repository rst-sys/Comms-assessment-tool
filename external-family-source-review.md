# Source review: External events and societal issues family

**Events in the family:** Geopolitical event (war, sanctions, unrest) · Natural disaster or extreme weather · Public health emergency · Social or political issue (deciding whether to speak)
**Scope:** United States and European Union
**Prepared:** 27 September 2026
**Purpose:** source basis for the External family protocol

---

## 0. How this review was built

This family already has the most groundwork. The geopolitical source review (`sources/reviews/geopolitical-event-source-review.md`, 22 September 2026) covers binding law, guidance, standards, codes and research for the hardest event in the family, and the core protocol review covers the public-health guidance (WHO, CDC). This review reuses both, reads one new source, and decides what lifts from the Geopolitical protocol to the family. Nothing is cited from memory.

---

## 1. The short answer

What the four events share is that **the organization didn't cause the event.** The account problem is not "what did you do?" but **"why are you speaking, what does it mean for your people and customers, and do your actions match your words?"** For disasters and health emergencies, add: **does what you tell people to do match the authorities?**

The geopolitical review's central finding holds for the whole family: *there is no recognised standard for how an organization should communicate about an event it didn't cause.* Binding law applies only through consequences (danger to workers, job losses, market disclosure), and those are already carried by the People harmed, Workforce impact and Listed company overlays. So the family rests on research and guidance applied by analogy, and says so.

Four sources carry the family:

- **Stances and actions (Vredenburg et al. 2020):** activism is authentic when a brand "match[es] activist messaging, purpose, and values with prosocial corporate practice"; when messaging is detached from practice, it is "woke washing", "potentially misleading consumers".
- **How stakeholders respond (Braga et al. 2026, meta-analysis):** a small positive effect overall (r = 0.084, 88 studies), strongest for consistent, resource-backed stances; **employees respond least favourably** of all stakeholder groups.
- **Why firms spoke on the Russia–Ukraine war (Bamiatzi et al. 2024):** peer imitation was one of four drivers, and some firms stayed "opportunistically neutral".
- **Consistent public-safety messages (WHO 2017, C4.2):** "Consistent messages should come from different information sources and emerge early on in the emergency" (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence).

## 2. The sources

### New in this review

**Vredenburg, J., Kapitan, S., Spry, A., & Kemper, J. A. (2020). "Brands Taking a Stand: Authentic Brand Activism or Woke Washing?" *Journal of Public Policy & Marketing*, 39(4), 444–460. doi:10.1177/0743915620947359.**
Link: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0743915620947359
*Read status:* opened, partly read (abstract).
*Key material:* "When brands match activist messaging, purpose, and values with prosocial corporate practice, they engage in authentic brand activism … In contrast, brands that detach their activist messaging from their purpose, values, and practice are enacting inauthentic brand activism through the practice of 'woke washing,' potentially misleading consumers with their claims."
*Weight:* a conceptual paper that builds a typology; it does not measure effects. Supports checking that words match practice; doesn't show that matching raises trust.

### Reused from the geopolitical review (already registered)

- **Braga, Tardin, Grinstein & Perin (2026), meta-analysis**, *Journal of Business Research* 210. Read (abstract, method, findings). 88 studies, 501 effect sizes, r = 0.084, I² = 98.9%; consistent, resource-backed and supportive stances work best; employees least favourable.
- **Bamiatzi et al. (2024)**, *Journal of Business Ethics* 198. Read (abstract, method, findings). Qualitative taxonomy of responses to the Russia–Ukraine war; peer imitation as a driver; "opportunistically neutral" firms.
- **Council Directive 89/391/EEC**, Art. 8: inform workers "as soon as possible" of serious and imminent danger and the protective steps. Already carried by the People harmed overlay and the Geopolitical protocol.
- **California Labor Code § 1137** (required meetings on political matters): current enforceability unverified. Used for a reviewer question only.

### Reused from the core review (already registered)

- **WHO (2017), *Communicating risk in public health emergencies*.** Read (recommendations, method). C4.2 on consistent messages from different sources; C4.3 "Messages should promote specific actions people can realistically take". Written for authorities; applied by analogy.
- **CDC CERC Manual, Introduction (2018 update).** Read (principles). "Promote Action"; "Be Right".

The Geopolitical protocol's eight checks were written for one event. Two are general to the family:

## 5. How good the evidence is

Weak, and the labels must say so. The meta-analysis finds a small effect with extreme heterogeneity; the taxonomy and the conceptual paper measure nothing; the WHO guidance was written for health authorities. None measures whether a message about an external event gave a credible account. The strongest point the research makes is a caution: **employees respond least favourably to corporate stances**, which argues for restraint, a stated connection and concrete action rather than declarations.

---

## 6. Known failure patterns

**Documented in a source**
1. Messaging detached from practice ("woke washing", Vredenburg 2020).
2. Speaking because peers did (Bamiatzi 2024).
3. Saying little while continuing as before, and letting silence read as principle (Bamiatzi 2024).

**Practitioner observation, not sourced (label as judgement)**
4. Solidarity with no connection, action or resource: "we stand with…", "our thoughts are with…".
5. A required step (an authority's closure order, sanctions compliance) presented as a moral choice or generosity.
6. Safety instructions that differ from the authorities' without saying so ("use your judgement about whether it is safe to come in" while an evacuation order is in force).
7. A donation announced with no amount, recipient or date.

---

## 7. Scope limits

- **Sanctions law**, national transposition of EU directives, US mini-WARN and captive-audience laws other than California's: not reviewed (as in the geopolitical review).
- **Public-health law** (for example workplace measures during an epidemic) and **disaster law** (evacuation and closure orders): not reviewed. The family asks that drafts match the authorities, not what the authorities may require.
- **Whether to speak at all on a social issue:** no source gives a rule. The family asks for a stated connection; it doesn't judge the position.

## 9. What nobody has established

1. When an organization has standing to speak on an event it didn't cause (unchanged from the geopolitical review).
2. Whether matching words to action raises trust, as opposed to avoiding a backlash.
3. What an organization owes employees who are on different sides of a social or political issue.

## Recommendation for the tool

Adopt the four checks in section 4 as the External family protocol, with four triggers and four reviewer questions. On geopolitical drafts, the Geopolitical protocol's *Basis for speaking* and *Exposure separated from event* replace the family's two general versions, and the family's compliance trigger gives way to the protocol's *Discretion inside compliance*.
