# Source review: Commercial and financial decisions family

**Events in the family:** Merger, acquisition or sale · Disappointing results or profit warning · Price increase or change to terms · Financial difficulty or cost-cutting · Change of strategy or exit from a market
**Scope:** United States and European Union
**Prepared:** 26 September 2026
**Purpose:** source basis for the Commercial family protocol

## 1. The short answer

None of the five events has its own protocol, so today they get only the core protocol, plus the Listed company overlay for listed organizations and the Workforce impact overlay when a merger, cost-cutting or market exit is addressed to employees.

What these events share is a business decision whose effects fall on customers, employees and investors, often described through forecasts. Three sources speak to that:

- **US forward-looking statements (15 U.S.C. § 78u-5):** a listed company's forecast is protected from liability only if it is "identified as a forward-looking statement, and is accompanied by meaningful cautionary statements" identifying factors that could make results differ. That protection doesn't apply to tender offers or going-private transactions.
- **EU changes to customer contracts (Directive 2018/1972, Article 105(4), telecoms):** customers must get at least a month's notice of changes to their contract, "in a clear and comprehensible manner," with their right to terminate "without incurring any further costs."
- **EU takeover bids (Directive 2004/25/EC):** the boards must inform employees' representatives "as soon as the bid has been made public" (Art. 6), and the target's board must publish its opinion, including "the effects of implementation of the bid on … employment" (Art. 9(5)).

Nothing found sets message content for profit warnings beyond the listed-company disclosure rules, or for financial difficulty.

## 2. The sources, by tier

### Tier 1 — Binding law

**1.1 Private Securities Litigation Reform Act safe harbor, 15 U.S.C. § 78u-5.**
Link: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/78u-5
*Read status:* opened, partly read ((b), (c)(1)(A)(i), (i)(1), as returned).
*Key material:* protection applies where a forward-looking statement is "identified as a forward-looking statement, and is accompanied by meaningful cautionary statements" identifying important factors that could cause actual results to differ materially. Excluded: statements made in connection with tender offers and going-private transactions. Forward-looking statements include projections of financial items, plans and objectives, future economic performance, and the assumptions underlying them.
*Binds:* it's a condition for protection, not a duty to disclose; it applies to issuers reporting to the SEC.

**1.2 Directive (EU) 2018/1972 (European Electronic Communications Code), Article 105(4).**
Link read: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2018/1972/article/105 (UK retained copy)
*Read status:* opened, partly read (Article 105(4)).
*Key material:* end-users "shall have the right to terminate their contract without incurring any further costs upon notice of changes in the contractual conditions" (with exceptions for changes that benefit them, are purely administrative, or are required by law); at least one month's notice; providers must inform them "in a clear and comprehensible manner on a durable medium" of the changes and of the right to terminate.
*Binds:* electronic communications providers in the EU, through transposition. **Sector-specific:** it doesn't govern price changes in other industries.

**1.3 Directive 2004/25/EC on takeover bids, Articles 6 and 9(5).**
Link read: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/LexUriServ/LexUriServ.do?uri=CELEX:32004L0025:EN:HTML
*Read status:* opened, partly read; quotes abbreviated by the tool.
*Key material:* Art. 6: "As soon as the bid has been made public, the boards of the offeree company and of the offeror shall inform the representatives of their respective employees or … the employees themselves" (abbreviated). Art. 9(5): the target's board "shall draw up and make public a document setting out its opinion of the bid … including its views on the effects of implementation of the bid on all the company's interests and specifically employment" (abbreviated).
*Binds:* member states, through transposition; takeover bids for EU-listed companies only.

## 4. How good the evidence is

The law is specific but narrow: forecasts only for US-listed companies, and only as a condition of legal protection; customer notice only for EU telecoms; employee information only for EU takeover bids. Applying them to other organizations, sectors and deals is professional judgement, and the labels say so. No research was reviewed for this family.

---

## 5. Known failure patterns

**Documented in a source**
1. Forecasts presented without the risks that could change them (the reason the US safe harbor requires "meaningful cautionary statements").
2. Contract changes announced without telling customers they can leave (the reason EECC Art. 105(4) requires the right to terminate to be stated).
3. Takeovers announced without telling employees what they mean for jobs (the reason Takeover Directive Arts. 6 and 9(5) exist).

**Practitioner observation, not sourced (label as judgement)**
4. "Synergies" or "savings" figures that are mostly job cuts, with no mention of jobs.
5. "Business as usual" or "nothing changes for customers" in a merger or exit announcement when integration or withdrawal is planned.
