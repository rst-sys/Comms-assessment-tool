# Source review: Leadership change family

**Events in the family:** CEO or senior leader departure · New CEO or leadership appointment · Board change or governance dispute · Death of a leader or employee
**Scope:** United States and European Union
**Prepared:** 26 September 2026
**Purpose:** source basis for the Leadership family protocol

---

## 0. How these sources were read

Same method as the earlier reviews. This family leans heavily on the CEO departure source review (`sources/ceo-departure-source-review.md`), which covers US securities law, the EU market-abuse rules on appointments and removals, and the research on succession announcements. Those sources are cross-referenced, not re-read. New sources were opened for appointments and deaths. Nothing is cited from memory.

---

## 1. The short answer

CEO departures already have a detailed protocol. **The gap is appointments, board disputes and deaths**, which get only the core protocol today.

For **listed companies in the US**, the law is specific about appointments and board disputes:

- **Appointments (Form 8-K Item 5.02(c)):** the company must disclose the new officer's name, position and date of appointment; "any arrangement or understanding … pursuant to which he was or is to be selected"; any family relationship with a director or executive officer (Regulation S-K Item 401); related-person transactions (Item 404(a)); and any material pay arrangement made in connection with the appointment.
- **New directors (Item 5.02(d)):** the same kind of arrangements, the committees they will join, and related-person transactions.
- **Board disputes (Item 5.02(a)):** when a director leaves over a disagreement, "a brief description of the circumstances representing the disagreement."

In the **EU**, the removal or appointment decision itself triggers disclosure for listed issuers (CEO review 2.6); nothing found specifies the content of an appointment announcement.

For **deaths**, no rule governs the announcement. SEC staff treat a death as outside the Form 8-K departure item (CEO review 2.9), and the GDPR "does not apply to the personal data of deceased persons," leaving it to member states. What a death announcement should say rests on professional judgement.

## 2. The sources, by tier

### Tier 1 — Binding law

**1.1 US SEC, Form 8-K, Item 5.02(a), (c) and (d)** (form revision SEC 873, February 2025).
Link: https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf
*Read status:* opened, partly read (the three sub-items, as returned). The CEO review could not get Item 5.02(a) and (b) verbatim; this fetch returned (a), (c) and (d) text.
*Key material:* (a) on a director's departure over a disagreement: "(i) the date of such resignation…; (ii) any positions held by the director on any committee…; and (iii) a brief description of the circumstances representing the disagreement." (c) on a new officer: "the name and position of the newly appointed officer and the date of the appointment; the information required by Items 401(b), (d), (e) and Item 404(a) of Regulation S-K," and "a brief description of any material plan, contract or arrangement … entered into or material amendment in connection with the triggering event." (d) on a new director: "a brief description of any arrangement or understanding between the new director and any other persons, naming such persons, pursuant to which such director was selected," the committees, and Item 404(a) information.
*Binds:* SEC reporting companies.

**1.2 US SEC, Regulation S-K Item 401, 17 CFR 229.401** (current as of 24 September 2026).
Link: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-229/subpart-229.400/section-229.401
*Read status:* opened, partly read ((b), (d), (e)).
*Key material:* (b) "describe briefly any arrangement or understanding between him and any other person(s) (naming such person) pursuant to which he was or is to be selected as an officer." (d) "State the nature of any family relationship between any director, executive officer, or person nominated or chosen by the registrant to become a director or executive officer," defined as "any relationship by blood, marriage, or adoption, not more remote than first cousin." (e) five years of business experience.

**1.3 GDPR, Recital 27.**
Link: https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-27/
*Read status:* opened and read.
*Key material:* "This Regulation does not apply to the personal data of deceased persons. Member States may provide for rules regarding the processing of personal data of deceased persons."
*Why it matters here:* what an employer says about a deceased colleague is governed by national rules, confidentiality duties and the family's wishes, not by EU data-protection law.

### Already reviewed (CEO departure review), relevant here

- **SEC C&DI 217.04:** death of a director or officer is not reportable under Item 5.02.
- **SEC Staff Legal Bulletin 14E:** CEO succession planning "raises a significant policy issue regarding the governance of the corporation."
- **EU Delegated Regulation 2026/789, Annex I row 13:** the decision on appointment or removal is the final event triggering disclosure for listed issuers.
- **Graffin, Carpenter & Boivie (2011):** unrelated news released alongside 20% of CEO successions, against an 11.4% baseline.
- **McConnell & Qi (2022), abstract only:** market reaction to in-depth succession-planning disclosure is positive for larger, stable firms and negative for smaller, less stable ones.

## 4. How good the evidence is

The law is strong and specific, but for US-listed companies only, and for filings rather than announcements. Applying it to press releases and internal memos, and to private, nonprofit and public organizations, is professional judgement, and the labels say so. EU coverage of appointment content is thin. Nothing found addresses death announcements.

---

## 5. Known failure patterns

**Documented in a source**
1. Burying a succession under unrelated news (Graffin et al. 2011).
2. Appointing someone with an undisclosed family or financial tie (the reason Items 401(d) and 404(a) exist).
3. A director leaving over a disagreement that is never described (the reason Item 5.02(a) exists).

**Practitioner observation, not sourced (label as judgement)**
4. An appointment announced as a biography with no word on who chose the person or how.
5. "Differences of opinion" or "a difference in strategic direction" with nothing on what the difference was.
6. A death announcement that speculates about cause, or shares private details the family hasn't agreed to.

## 6. Scope limits

- **Nonprofits, public bodies, private companies:** no rules reviewed; their governance documents and state or national law apply.
- **EU national governance codes** beyond Germany's (CEO review) not reviewed; the UK is out of scope.
- **Regulation S-K Item 404(a)** (related-person transactions) referenced by Item 5.02 but not opened.
- **Research on appointment or death announcements:** none found or reviewed.
