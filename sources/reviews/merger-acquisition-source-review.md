# Source review: Merger or acquisition event protocol (gun jumping)

**Event:** Merger, acquisition or sale (id `merger-acquisition`, Commercial family)
**Scope:** United States and European Union
**Prepared:** 28 September 2026
**Purpose:** source basis for a Merger or acquisition event protocol, focused on "gun jumping": communicating or acting as if a deal has closed before it legally can

---

## 0. How these sources were read

Same method as the earlier reviews. Regulators' own pages were read where they could be opened; where only a law-firm or court-watch summary was available, it says so. Law-firm summaries are used for the facts of cases, never as authority for what the law requires. Nothing is cited from memory.

---

## 1. The short answer

Between signing a deal and closing it, the two companies must stay separate. In the US, under the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Act, "The parties may not close their deal until the waiting period outlined in the HSR Act has passed, or the government has granted early termination" (FTC). In the EU, a notifiable deal "shall not be implemented either before its notification or until it has been declared compatible with the common market" (European Commission, stating Article 7(1) of the Merger Regulation). Acting as one company early is "gun jumping", and it is enforced:

- **US, January 2025:** three oil companies paid **$5.6 million**, "the largest dollar penalty ever imposed for a gun-jumping violation in U.S. history", for coordinating customer contracts, deliveries and pricing, and halting the target's drilling, before closing (FTC).
- **EU, 2018; upheld November 2023:** Altice was fined **€124.5 million** for implementing its acquisition of PT Portugal before approval. The Court of Justice held that implementation "arises as soon as … operations contributing to a lasting change in the control of the target undertaking" are made (Case C-746/21 P, as reported).

Communications matter because they are both **evidence** and **conduct**. A practitioner analysis for the American Bar Association warned against "holding out the companies as having merged before the merger is complete", and the government's Gemstar-TV Guide case involved parties agreeing "to phase out competing marketing operations, to allocate customers, and to agree on prices" before closing. A memo that tells the target's staff to route decisions through the acquirer, or tells customers they now deal with one company, can itself be the violation.

Separately, US securities rules require written communications about a business combination by SEC-registered companies to be filed "on the date of first use" (Rule 425). That's a process duty, not message content, so it belongs in a reviewer question.

**This passes the framework's promotion test** (event-specific law; documented failure pattern; three or more event-specific checks), so M&A should get its own **event protocol**, not more checks in the Commercial family.

Link read: https://www.ftc.gov/advice-guidance/competition-guidance/guide-antitrust-laws/mergers/premerger-notification-merger-review-process
*Read status:* opened and read (the FTC page; the statute itself not opened).
*Key material:* "The parties may not close their deal until the waiting period outlined in the HSR Act has passed, or the government has granted early termination of the waiting period."
*Binds:* parties to deals above the HSR thresholds.

**1.2 US Federal Trade Commission, "Oil Companies to Pay Record FTC Gun-Jumping Fine for Antitrust Law Violation", press release, January 2025.**
Link read: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/oil-companies-pay-record-ftc-gun-jumping-fine-antitrust-law-violation
*Read status:* opened and read (as summarised by the tool).
*Key material:* "It is generally illegal to finalize an acquisition during this investigatory period"; XCL, Verdun and EP coordinated customer contracts, deliveries and pricing and halted EP's well-drilling before closing; $5.6 million, "the largest dollar penalty ever imposed for a gun-jumping violation in U.S. history"; the violation ran for 94 days in 2021.

**1.3 Council Regulation (EC) No 139/2004 (EU Merger Regulation), Article 7(1), as stated by the European Commission, "Merger procedures".**
Link read: https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/mergers/procedures_en
*Read status:* opened and read (the Commission page; the Regulation's text on EUR-Lex not reached).
*Key material:* notifiable concentrations "shall not be implemented either before its notification or until it has been declared compatible with the common market pursuant to a Commission decision."
*Binds:* deals with an EU dimension.

**1.4 Court of Justice of the EU, Altice Group Lux v Commission, Case C-746/21 P, judgment of 9 November 2023.**
Link read: https://www.clearyantitrustwatch.com/2023/11/european-court-of-justice-confirms-altices-gun-jumping-violation/ (a law firm's report; the judgment and the Commission's 2018 press release were not read in full)
*Read status:* secondary.
*Key material, as reported:* implementation "arises as soon as […] operations contributing to a lasting change in the control of the target undertaking" are made; "any operation conferring the 'possibility' of exercising decisive influence" counts. The Commission's 2018 fine of €124.5 million was upheld (the fine confirmed from the Commission press release's metadata).

**1.5 SEC Rule 425, 17 CFR 230.425.**
Link read: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-230/subject-group-ECFRb0116042526d15d/section-230.425
*Read status:* opened, partly read (paragraphs (a) and (b), as summarised).
*Key material:* written communications made in reliance on Rule 165 "must be filed with the Commission under this section on the date of first use"; communications under Rule 135 must be filed "on or before the date of first use".
*Binds:* SEC-registered companies in business combinations.

### Tier 6 — Practitioner analysis

**Liebeskind, R., "Gun-jumping: Antitrust Issues Before Closing the Merger", American Bar Association presentation, August 2003.**
Link read: https://www.pillsburylaw.com/images/content/1/8/v2/1842/16ADC9E2C53CF6E9F97E3F0A3F6F3242.pdf

*Weight:* an experienced practitioner's analysis; dated 2003 but consistent with the 2018 and 2025 enforcement. Used for what communications have been treated as risky, not as authority for the law.

## 4. How good the evidence is

The law is clear, binding and recently enforced at record levels on both sides of the Atlantic. What the law governs is *conduct*: implementing a deal early. Messages are relevant as evidence of that conduct and as instructions that are themselves conduct. No source sets rules for what a deal announcement must say; applying the law to message content is professional judgement, informed by a practitioner analysis and the facts of enforced cases.

---

## 5. Known failure patterns

**Documented in a source**
1. Operating as one company before closing: coordinating pricing, customers and deliveries (FTC 2025).
2. Acquirer controlling the target's decisions before approval (Altice).
3. Holding out as merged; phasing out competing marketing; allocating customers (Liebeskind 2003; Gemstar).

**Practitioner observation, not sourced (label as judgement)**
4. "Welcome to the [acquirer] family" memos to target staff that also tell them to take direction from the acquirer.
5. Customer letters signed jointly as one company before closing.
6. Announcing post-closing leaders, prices or product decisions as already in effect.
